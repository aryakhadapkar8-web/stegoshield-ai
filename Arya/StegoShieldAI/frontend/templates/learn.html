🧠 WHAT IS STEGANOGRAPHY?

Steganography (derived from the Greek words steganos meaning "covered" and graphein meaning "writing") is the art and science of hiding secret data inside an innocent-looking cover medium (such as an image, audio track, video file, or text document).

Unlike cryptography—which scrambles messages to render them unreadable—steganography hides the very existence of the secret communication. An observer sees only an ordinary picture or sound file.

📜 HISTORY OF STEGANOGRAPHY

  • Ancient Greece (440 BC): Demaratus sent secret messages by shaving a slave's head, tattooing the message on the scalp, and letting the hair regrow.
  • Invisible Inks (WW I & II): Milk, lemon juice, or chemical solutions used to write messages between lines of normal letters.
  • Microdots (WW II): Photos scaled down to the size of a typewriter period and embedded in regular letters.
  • Modern Digital Steganography: LSB substitution in digital RGB pixels, audio PCM samples, and zero-width unicode characters.

📁 TYPES OF DIGITAL STEGANOGRAPHY

1. Image Steganography

Modifies the least significant bits (LSB) of RGB pixel channels (0-255). Changes of ±1 pixel intensity are imperceptible to human eyes.

2. Audio Steganography

Injects bits into 16-bit PCM audio sample values in WAV files. Masked by psychoacoustic hearing thresholds.

3. Video Steganography

Spreads secret payloads across video frames (24-60 fps), providing huge embedding capacity for large files.

4. Text Steganography

Utilizes zero-width spaces (\u200B, \u200C) inserted invisibly into ordinary plain text strings.

✅ ADVANTAGES

  • Covert communication prevents eavesdroppers from flagging suspicious traffic.
  • Hybrid AES-256 + LSB provides multi-layered defense.
  • Visually lossless quality (PSNR > 40 dB).
  • Reusable across images, audio, video, and text.

⚠️ LIMITATIONS

  • Lossy compression (JPEG, MP3) can destroy LSB hidden data.
  • Payload size is constrained by cover media capacity.
  • Advanced AI steganalysis detectors can identify statistical anomalies.
